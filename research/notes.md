# Source notes — November 18, 2026

## Brief

- **Title:** Accountant marketplace built on standardized AI testing
- **Public URL:** https://www.ideabrowser.com/hub/ideas/today
- **IB Index:** 5.5; B2B SaaS; high difficulty.
- **Economics:** $80K–$180K year one; $2M–$4M ARR ceiling; $15K–$40K initial capital; 60–120 days to first revenue.
- **Demand:** 131K aggregate monthly searches cited (+82% YoY), led by “CPA near me” at 90.5K/mo; “bookkeeping services” 18.1K/mo (+235%); “AI for accountants” 6.6K and flat; “QuickBooks ProAdvisor” 5.4K (+23%). Exact buyer intent for an AI-tested directory is weak: the report cites only 390 searches/mo for “find an accountant,” 50 for “accountant directory,” and zero for “AI certification accounting.”

## Customer and painful status quo

The nominal buyer is an independent accountant/small firm paying for qualified introductions; the end user is a US small-business owner selecting a provider. Owners report miscategorizations, missed deadlines, slow replies, and cycling through firms. Their actual desired outcome is reliable books and responsiveness—not an AI badge.

## Gap, wedge, and why now

Free directories show credentials and location but do not compare real work, AI disclosure, human review, or data protection. The proposed wedge is one synthetic business record set: every firm cleans identical books, answers identical client questions, documents review, and explains privacy. A qualified reviewer publishes an expiring scorecard.

The report says the AICPA and AIUC launched a joint SOC 2 + AIUC-1 assurance program on October 19, 2026, with broader launch expected in early 2027. Thomson Reuters research cited 78% of corporate clients calling AI-enabled quality essential/very important, only 6% believing providers deliver, and 32% reconsidering providers. This is stronger upmarket evidence than small-business search demand.

## Competitor map

- **Discovery:** QuickBooks ProAdvisor (500K+ listings), Xero Advisor Directory, CPAdirectory (600K+ CPAs), Thumbtack.
- **Verification:** CPAverify and the profession-owned AICPA/AIUC assurance framing.
- **Direct substitutes:** Collective, Pilot, Bench, plus AI-native firms such as Bear, Balance, and Rational. They bypass directories by selling the service directly.

## Offer ladder and GTM

Report ladder: free public test methodology; $750 standardized assessment; $149/mo qualified-intro retainer; $250 retest and $299/mo featured placement. The stronger pivot is a testing media property: publish “We tested 10 AI accounting firms on the same books,” monetize affiliate intros, assurance-prep workshops, or methodology licensing. Recruit the first 10 firms through direct outreach to AI-native/YC firms and accounting communities. Separate editorial scoring from paid placement.

Napkin assumptions: 50 firms contacted, 20% close, 10 assessments at $750, half convert to $149/mo, about $9K booked in 90 days and $745 MRR exiting day 90. These are assumptions, not observed conversion.

## Risks and build implication

The largest risk is firms refusing public grades, especially when the profession’s own credential may dominate. A startup reviewer also needs CPA credibility and careful legal positioning. FirmTrial therefore prototypes a transparent scoring workbench—not a live directory—and marks all evidence fictional. No simulated AI is used; scoring is deterministic and inspectable.
