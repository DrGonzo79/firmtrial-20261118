# CEO review

## Challenge

The original marketplace premise confuses visible owner pain with demand for an AI badge. Free directories own discovery, direct AI firms bypass comparison, and the profession’s own assurance program can out-credential a startup. $149/month intros also contaminates score credibility. The attractive $2M–$4M ceiling is irrelevant until firms consent, pay, and buyers act.

The credible wedge is private readiness assessment plus a testing media property. Even that demands a named CPA reviewer; software alone has no moat. A public score can create legal and reputational risk, and a weighted number invites false precision unless every score exposes rubric version, evidence, flags, reviewer, and expiry.

## Decisions applied

- Prototype the same-case scoring operation, not listings or lead routing.
- Make scoring deterministic, editable, and explainable; no fake AI.
- Use only fictional firms/evidence and repeat the credential disclaimer.
- Penalize explicit exceptions and expose every criterion in the detail view.
- Keep Python deployable/tested but unhosted; Pages is frontend-only.
- Set the go/no-go around three paid $750 pilots and publication consent, not traffic.
- Separate editorial scoring from any future paid placement and include an appeal trail in the production architecture.

## Failure modes to instrument

Rubric gaming, reviewer disagreement, score drift after rubric changes, missing consent, expired evidence, artifact leakage, cross-tenant access, model overreach, low publication consent, and assessment labor destroying margin. If the test remains a labor-heavy private consultancy, price it accordingly instead of forcing SaaS economics.
